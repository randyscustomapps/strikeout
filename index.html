How it works it out

Your GPS lines are spaced at your header width. Everyone's cut counts as header width minus overlap.

The app shows whichever way needs the smaller nudge. The other way is listed in the box above.

How to use it

My header stays set until you change it. 1. Other combines is everyone in the crew besides you who'll cut this land: the ones out with you now, or the ones you want to leave room for.

2. Following me / Not following. Following me shows two compact switches: Following on Right/Left (usually Right), and Strike out Skip/Outside. Skip jumps over the pack (followers out + whole crew back). Outside keeps crop on the outside — leave room for the followers only (e.g. four 40′ headers → +5), which also handles mixed header sizes. Not following means you're going out alone and only leaving room for the other combines in the headland (not yourself). Three others same size → +4. Two → +3. Nobody else → +1.

3. Turning at the end: pick the way you'll turn, as you're driving the pass you're on now. Heading north and turning east to strike out and come back south? That's Right.

4. Count from the line you last struck out on, not the line you just came back on. That pass is always the edge of what's left to cut. At the start of a field, count from your line along the edge. Last strike-out on 16 and it says +10? Go to 26. (If your line numbers count down that way, go to 6. Just move 10 lines over.)

5. Quick check with every header the same size as yours: Following me + Skip = 2 × the number of combines, you included. Outside = others + 1 (e.g. four → +5). Not following = others + 1. If followers are wider than you, Skip adds one extra of your lines so you don’t land a pass short — trust the number on the card.

6. The nudge is left or right after the turn, once you're lined up on the new line (heading south in that example), so it matches the arrows on your GPS. Farther out means away from the land you've cut, and back in means toward it. Add it to whatever nudge is already dialled in.
